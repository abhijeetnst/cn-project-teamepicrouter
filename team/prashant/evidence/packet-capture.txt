### C1 – DNS (filter: `dns`) – Draft

Filter `dns`. **Query:** "Standard query A app.teamepicrouter.test" from the client 172.31.250.14, source port 58961
(an ephemeral port chosen by the client), to our DNS server 172.31.250.11 on UDP port 53.
**Response:** from 172.31.250.11:53 back to 172.31.250.14:58961, flags show a response with no error;
ANSWER: app.teamepicrouter.test A 172.31.250.12, TTL 30 s. 172.31.250.12 is the edge (node-2), so the client will connect
there next. The TTL tells the client it may cache this answer for 30 seconds.

### C2 – TCP three-way handshake (filter: `tcp.flags.syn==1`, then follow the stream) – Draft

**SYN** from the client 172.31.250.14:38762 (ephemeral port) to 172.31.250.12:443 (HTTPS),
initial sequence number 2346661895 (Wireshark shows it relative as Seq=0).
**SYN-ACK** from 172.31.250.12:443: the server's own ISN 3867343306 and Ack=2346661896 (client ISN + 1, relative Ack=1).
**ACK** from the client: Seq=1, Ack=1 – the connection is established before any TLS data is sent.
The handshake gives a reliable, ordered, connection-oriented channel: both sides agree on starting
sequence numbers so lost or out-of-order data can be detected and resent.

### C3 – TLS handshake (filter: `tls`) – Draft

Two requests were captured: one forced to TLS 1.2 (certificate visible) and one with TLS 1.3.
**ClientHello** from 172.31.250.14 to 172.31.250.12:443 with SNI app.teamepicrouter.test, offering 28 cipher suites and
TLS 1.3 / 1.2 as supported versions. **ServerHello** from 172.31.250.12 chooses TLS_AES_256_GCM_SHA384.
In the TLS 1.2 handshake the **Certificate** message is readable: subject CN=app.teamepicrouter.test,
SAN app.teamepicrouter.test and api.teamepicrouter.test, issued by "teamepicrouter Private CA". Then **ChangeCipherSpec** – from
here on both sides encrypt. Everything after that is shown only as **Application Data** (12 records):
the HTTP method, path, headers and JSON body are inside encrypted TLS records, so Wireshark cannot
read them without the session keys. (In TLS 1.3 even the certificate is already encrypted.)
